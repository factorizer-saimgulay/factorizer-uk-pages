One-off fabrication to EN 1090-2 EXC3 on a series line

What execution class 3 asks of a fabricator, how a single frame or cradle is made to it on a line built for series work, and what a buyer should expect to see in the record.

Published
17 November 2026
Solution
Contract Steel Fabrication

A transport frame, a blade cradle or a machine base is often a single piece: one drawing, one part, made once. The structures that carry wind components, rail vehicles or heavy machinery are nevertheless safety-critical, and the specification calls for EN 1090-2 at execution class 3. This note explains what that class asks for, and how a one-off is made to it in a production environment certified for the work.

What EN 1090-2 EXC3 means

EN 1090-2 sets the technical requirements for the execution of steel structures. The execution class, from EXC1 to EXC4, scales the requirements to the consequence of failure and the demands of the structure. EXC3 is the class for structures where failure would have serious consequences and where the welding and the fabrication are demanding: it is the usual class for frames that carry heavy loads over people and plant.

At EXC3 the standard asks, among other things, for:

  • Welding carried out under a welding quality system to EN ISO 3834-2, the comprehensive level.
  • Welders and welding operators qualified for the processes and the joints used.
  • Welding procedure specifications qualified by test, and a responsible welding coordinator.
  • Traceability of the material: the steel grade and the certificate behind each part.
  • Defined tolerances for the fabricated structure, and inspection to show they were met.
  • A record of all of it, so that the structure’s history can be followed from the certificate to the delivered part.

Why the production environment matters

A one-off is made on the same floor, by the same people and under the same system as series work. That is the point: the standards belong to the production environment, not to the part. The environment in which our fabrication is manufactured is certified to EN 1090-2 EXC3 and EN ISO 3834-2, and runs under ISO 9001, ISO 14001 and ISO 45001. A buyer of a single frame gets the welding coordination, the qualified procedures and the material traceability that the series work carries, because the system does not know the difference between one part and a hundred.

From the drawing to the part

The route for a one-off is the same route series work follows, with the series steps collapsed:

  1. Drawing review. The drawing is checked for the welds it calls for, the tolerances, the material and the coating, and anything that cannot be made as drawn is raised before steel is cut.
  2. Material. Profiles and plate are bought against the grade on the drawing, with certificates, and marked so each piece stays traceable.
  3. Cutting and preparation. Edges are prepared for the welds the procedure specifies.
  4. Welding. MIG, TIG or robotic welding, each under its qualified procedure. A one-off part is often welded by hand where a series part would be welded by a robot; the procedure is qualified either way.
  5. Inspection. Visual inspection of every weld, with further testing where the drawing or the class calls for it, and dimensional inspection against the tolerances.
  6. Coating. Hot-dip galvanising, liquid coating or metallising, to the drawing and to the place the part will live.
  7. The record. Material certificates, welder and procedure qualifications, inspection reports and the declaration of performance, delivered with the part.

What to expect in the record

A buyer specifying EXC3 should expect, and ask for, the material certificates tied to the parts, the welding procedure specifications used, the welder qualifications, the inspection reports and the final declaration. If any of these is missing the class has not been met, whatever the quality of the welds.

Steel, stainless and aluminium

The same route applies to stainless steel and to aluminium, each with its own qualified procedures. Transport frames are steel or aluminium, chosen for the component and the route; cradles and bases are usually carbon steel, galvanised or coated.

What we ask for

  • The drawing, with the execution class, the material and the coating stated.
  • The quantity, and whether it will repeat.
  • The delivery place and date.
  • Any inspection beyond the standard that the end client requires.
