Responsibility

Evidence, not intentions

Environmental and ethical claims are the easiest thing on a website to write and the hardest to stand behind. This page holds to the same rule as the rest of the site: a certificate, a design decision or a measurement. Where none of those exists yet, it says so instead of reaching for a phrase.

The machine

Where the environmental argument actually is.

It is not in a pledge. It is in what the machine burns, what it recovers, how many machines it replaces and how long it stays in service.

No engine, and nothing burned where it works

The machine is fully electric. There is no exhaust, no fuel spill risk and no engine noise at the point of work, which is what makes it usable indoors, in a shed and beside people.

Braking returns energy instead of wasting it

Braking is regenerative, so energy spent climbing comes back on the way down rather than leaving as heat in a brake disc. On sloping ground that is the difference between a duty cycle that fits in a battery and one that does not.

One machine that becomes many

What the platform is on any given day is decided by the attachment on its deck. A yard that can change the attachment does not need to buy, house and eventually scrap a separate machine for each job.

Built to be kept, not replaced

Spares, diagnostics, maintenance and technical support continue after handover. The environmental cost of a machine is dominated by making it, so the longer a machine stays in service the better that arithmetic gets.

One thing that follows from this and is worth stating plainly: electric at the point of work is not the same as clean overall. The electricity comes from a grid we do not control, and the battery has an origin and an end of life. Those are real and they belong to the customer as much as to us, which is why we would rather set them out than leave them implied.

People

Safety is the reason, not a side effect.

The work nobody should be doing

The case for taking a person out of a cab is strongest where the work is repetitive, continuous, or hazardous enough that being there is the risk. That is where these machines are aimed, and it is a safety argument before it is a cost one.

Handing control back cleanly

An unmanned machine has to be supervisable by someone who is not an engineer, and has to give control back without surprising the person taking it. How that handover works is designed, not left to a manual.

Removing a person is not the same as removing a job

We are not in a position to promise what an operator does instead, because that is the customer’s decision and not ours. What we can say is that we do not sell headcount reduction as the headline, and we will say so in a specification if asked.

Required

The floor the work has to be built on.

These two are the minimum criteria set for the production chain that builds our systems. They are held and audited where the work is actually done, they can be evidenced on request, and they are the reason this page is not built out of adjectives.

ISO 14001:2015
Environmental management: how environmental impact is identified and controlled.
ISO 45001:2018
Occupational health and safety: how risk to people at work is managed.

The full set required of that production environment, including quality, welding and structural execution, is on the company page.

Conduct

How we deal with people we do business with.

We say what a system will and will not do before an order is placed, including where we think automation is the wrong answer. A specification that oversells is a commissioning argument waiting to happen, and it costs both sides more than the honest version would have.

Personal data is handled the same way. We ask before we use it, we use it only for what we asked about, and we do not sell it or trade it. Nothing optional runs on this site until you switch it on. The privacy policy and the cookie policy set that out in full.

Not claimed

What is deliberately missing from this page.

Because the honest version of a responsibility page includes the parts that have not been measured yet.

  • No carbon figure, for the company or for a machine. We have not measured one, and an unmeasured number is worse than none.
  • No net-zero date. A date without a costed plan behind it is a press release.
  • No recycled-content or end-of-life recovery percentage for the battery or the structure.
  • No supplier code of conduct published here yet, and no audit programme behind one.

If your procurement process needs any of these, say so early. We would rather work out what can genuinely be evidenced for your tender than write something here that would not survive being asked about. Write to info@factorizer.uk.
