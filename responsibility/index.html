Responsibility

What we require, of the machine and of everyone who builds it

We make machines that take people out of repetitive and hazardous work, and we build them through a production chain held to defined standards. This page sets out what those standards are: the environmental decisions designed into the machine, the certifications the production environment has to hold, and the labour conditions we require of everyone in the chain.

The machine

The environmental decisions are made in the design.

Electric drive, energy recovered under braking, one platform that does the work of several machines, and a service life long enough to pay back the cost of making it.

Fully electric where it works

The machine is fully electric: zero exhaust, zero fuel handling and quiet running at the point of work, so it runs indoors, under cover and alongside people.

Braking that returns energy

Braking is regenerative, so energy spent climbing comes back to the battery on the way down. On sloping ground that keeps the duty cycle well within what the battery provides.

One machine that becomes many

What the platform is on any given day is decided by the attachment on its deck. One machine covers many jobs, so a site buys, houses and maintains fewer of them.

Built to be kept

Spares, diagnostics, maintenance and technical support continue after handover. Most of the environmental cost of a machine is incurred in making it, so a machine that stays in service is the one that pays that cost back.

The production chain

The floor every supplier has to meet.

These are the minimum criteria for the production environment our systems are built in, and every piece of work we place meets them. Certificates are held where the work is done and can be evidenced on request.

ISO 9001:2015
Quality management: how work is controlled, recorded and repeated consistently.
ISO 14001:2015
Environmental management: how environmental impact is identified and controlled.
ISO 45001:2018
Occupational health and safety: how risk to people at work is managed.
EN ISO 3834-2
Comprehensive quality requirements for fusion welding of metallic materials.
EN 1090-2 EXC3
Execution of steel structures at execution class 3, applied to demanding structural work.

Applied

Quality, environment, occupational health and safety, welding and structural execution. Five standards covering the whole way the work is controlled, from the process to the finished part.

Labour and modern slavery

Conditions of doing business with us.

Modern slavery, exploitative recruitment and unsafe working conditions are risks that sit in supply chains. These are the terms we require of ours, and we expect the same of anyone who supplies into it.

Employment is lawful and voluntary

Everyone working on our systems, in our production chain or on our sites does so under a lawful contract they entered freely and can leave. Wages, hours, rest and leave meet or exceed the law of the country the work is done in.

No forced labour, in any form

No forced, bonded, indentured or prison labour anywhere in the chain. No worker pays a fee to get a job, and no worker has identity documents, wages or possessions withheld to keep them in one.

No child labour

No one under the legal minimum working age, and no one under 18 in hazardous work. Age is verified at engagement.

Freedom to speak up

Workers are free to associate, to organise and to raise a concern, including about us, and are fully protected when they do.

We work with suppliers who meet these terms. Anyone with a concern about conditions anywhere in our chain can write to info@factorizer.uk, anonymously if they prefer.

Environment

Fewer machines to make, longer lives, material that comes back.

Environmental management is a condition of supply

ISO 14001 sits in the required set for the production environment our systems are built in. Identifying and controlling environmental impact is part of the price of doing the work.

Designed so there is less to make

One platform that changes job with its attachment replaces several single-purpose machines, so far less material and energy goes into making them.

Steel, and what happens to it afterwards

Structural work is fabricated to EN 1090-2 EXC3. Steel built to a known execution class can be assessed, repaired and, at the end of a long life, recycled as a material.

Kept running for the long term

Long-term service, spares and diagnostics exist so that a fault is a repair. Extending the working life of a delivered machine is the largest environmental decision available to us after the decision to make it electric.

People

The safety case is the reason the machine exists.

The strongest case for taking a person out of a cab is safety: work that is repetitive, continuous, or hazardous enough that being there is itself the risk. That is where these machines are aimed, and occupational health and safety management sits in the required set for the environment they are built in.

An unmanned machine is supervised by operators of every background, and hands control back smoothly and predictably to the person taking it. That handover is designed in from the start, because the moment control changes hands is where a safety case proves itself.
