Vehicles

Electric and autonomous industrial vehicles

These vehicles are our own technology. The platform, the electric powertrain, the autonomous and remote controlled operation and the operator interface are designed and built by us, as one system rather than bolted together late. They are not bought in and rebadged, and we are not converting a machine that somebody else built.

The platform itself is sector-agnostic. It is fully electric, it brakes regeneratively so energy spent climbing is recovered coming down, and it is built for heavy loads on broken ground and gradients rather than on a level slab. What it is on any given day is decided by what is fitted to the deck: change the attachment and it becomes that machine.

It runs on its own, in a pair under a load neither could take alone, or as a fleet working to one plan.

In aircraft assembly it is not only how a section is moved, it is what the section is built on. A fuselage can stay on the same platform from the start of production to the end of it, and be taken between stations with the precision the next operation needs, instead of being lifted off and set down again.

  • Carries a workpiece through production, not just between buildings
  • Precision positioning between stations
  • Fully electric, with regenerative braking
  • Heavy payload, carried low and flat on the deck
  • Attachment-defined: the deck decides what the machine is
  • Broken ground, uneven surfaces and gradients
  • Works alone, in a pair, or as a coordinated fleet
  • Autonomous or remote controlled operation
  • After-sales service, spares and remote diagnostics for the life of the machine

Who it suits

Operators whose work is repetitive, continuous or hazardous enough that removing the person from the cab changes how the whole operation is organised.

Cells

Robotic and automation cells

Turnkey cells for welding, palletising, machine tending and comparable applications. Each cell is simulated and validated before manufacture, then built, integrated and commissioned by the same team.

Robot choice follows the application rather than the other way round, so the cell is specified before a brand is.

  • Welding cells and welding cabinets
  • Palletising and depalletising
  • Machine tending, cobot applications and deburring
  • Gripping and EOAT systems matched to the part

Who it suits

Manufacturers with a process that is well understood but hard to staff consistently, where the constraint is throughput or repeatability rather than machine capability.

Fabrication

Contract steel fabrication

We manufacture and deliver fabricated steel parts to order, in the same production environment and to the same standards as the systems we build for ourselves.

  • Fabricated and welded steel parts made to drawing
  • Structural fabrication to EN 1090-2 EXC3
  • Welding to EN ISO 3834-2
  • Delivered as finished parts, not as a system

Who it suits

Work that has to be made to a standard the customer can evidence, where the fabrication itself is the deliverable.

Enterprise software for project work

Cluster ERP

Cluster ERP is our own modular enterprise resource planning software, built for companies whose work is driven by projects and production rather than by stock movements alone. It covers the ground a production business needs covered, with the eBOM, MRP and MES at the centre of it, and because we build it ourselves it can be shaped around how a business actually runs.

What modular means in practice

A project- and production-driven company rarely needs every part of an ERP on day one, and rarely needs them in the same order. Modular means the system is adopted in stages: start with the area causing the most pain, run it properly, then extend into the next area without replacing what is already working.

Modules share one data foundation, so extending the system adds capability rather than adding another set of records to reconcile.

Module areas

  • eBOM: the engineering bill of materials everything else is built from
  • Project and works order management
  • MRP: material requirements and production planning
  • MES: shop floor execution and tracking
  • Procurement, inventory and materials
  • Quality, traceability and documentation
  • Costing, commercial and reporting

Who it suits

Engineering and manufacturing businesses running to order, where jobs are individually costed and generic stock-led ERP forces the work into the wrong shape.

Delivery

From requirement to running fleet.

Every programme follows the same route, whatever the sector. The stages below are carried by one organisation, so the people who set the requirement are the people answering for it at commissioning.

  1. 01

    Requirements

    We start in the operating environment, not in the specification document. The duty, the constraints, the safety picture and the people involved are established before any design decision is fixed.

  2. 02

    Design and simulation

    Concept and detailed design are developed and then validated digitally. Cells and processes are simulated so reach, cycle, layout and collision questions are resolved before steel is cut.

  3. 03

    Manufacture and integration

    Systems are manufactured in a production environment certified to ISO 9001:2015, ISO 14001:2015, ISO 45001:2018, EN ISO 3834-2 and EN 1090-2 EXC3. Mechanical build, robotics, controls and interfaces are integrated as one system.

  4. 04

    Commissioning and operation

    We commission on site and hand over a system proven in its real environment. From that point it is backed by long-term service, maintenance and operational support.
